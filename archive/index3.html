Act 1

Daring to build

Every age is remembered by what it built, with new infrastructure transforming the way the world works.

Past eras have been defined by canals, railways, telephone lines, highways and internet cables.

Each made possible ways of living and kinds of growth that no one could have imagined before.

The next age will run on electricity, and it is arriving fast.

Its success depends on whether we build the infrastructure it runs on. That choice is being made now.

Act 2

The world moved on, the grid did not

How we work, travel and communicate has changed beyond recognition in the last fifty years.

But the grid has stayed fundamentally the same.

Grids are out of step with a world that moves faster and is hungrier for power than any before.

Electrostates

Across the world, countries are transforming themselves into electrostates: economies that run on electricity from the ground up.

In these countries, cars, trains, kitchens, heating and factories are switching from fossil fuels to clean electricity.

Domestic wind and solar are replacing fossil fuel imports.

Data centres are expanding, drawing power around the clock.

The nations that build a grid for this age of power will be where AI, industry and investment go.

Act 3

A new type of grid, fit for the age of power

Fossil power Clean electricity From storage High-capacity flow Flexible flow (both directions)

The old grid sent power one way, from a few large power stations to everyone else.

In a modern grid, generation is increasingly spread across the country

and power flows now moves in two ways. Homes and businesses draw electricity from the grid and send it back in.

The system no longer depends on a few big plants and becomes more resilient.

This is enabled by smart devices and digitalisation, which allow us to see and control what is happening on the grid.

When power is plentiful and cheap, electric vehicles charge and heating or cooling turns on.

Batteries store surplus electricity and release it when generation falls.

Bills fall, the system runs better and we can do more, faster with the existing grid.

The wires are smarter too. Lines fitted with sensors carry more power when the weather allows,

and advanced cables made from new, lighter materials carry much more on the same towers.

This is the modern grid: digital, flexible and smarter about how it grows. It gets more out of the lines we already have.

New lines are planned and built using the technology of today, rather than the designs of decades ago.

The connection queues holding back investment start to clear and projects get online quicker, without waiting for a new line to be built.

This is not the old grid made bigger. It is a modern grid, fit for the age of power.

Modernising the grid

A host of solutions to achieve this already exist.

Sorting the 23 signs

Put every intervention on two axes: how fast it delivers, and how much it costs.

A pattern appears. Cheap things tend to be fast. Expensive things tend to be slow. Quick wins are available now, and the most transformative changes need the most capital.

Two surprises

Battery storage is now a quick win, not a distant bet. Demand response is low-cost, just a little slower to scale.

The age of power is here.

The only question is where it will flourish. That will be wherever the grid is ready to carry it.
